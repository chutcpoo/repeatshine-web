REPEATSHINE

Mobile detailing slow bookings? Start with past customers.

When the calendar has gaps, the fastest list to inspect is usually your own customer history. The goal is not to blast everyone. It is to find the people who are actually due for service and give yourself a clear follow-up queue.

A simple recovery plan

  1. 1. Pull the last 6–12 months of completed jobs.
    Start with customer, vehicle, last-service date, service type, and ticket value. Do not worry about building a perfect CRM first.
  2. 2. Estimate a sensible next-service date.
    Use the service type and the interval you normally recommend. Customers with no usable date go into a separate cleanup list.
  3. 3. Work overdue and due-soon customers first.
    A short priority list is more useful than scrolling through every past customer.
  4. 4. Send a personal check-in.
    Mention the vehicle or last service. Avoid fake urgency or a generic discount blast.
  5. 5. Record the outcome.
    Mark rebooked, not now, no response, or wrong timing so tomorrow's list gets smaller instead of resetting.

A low-pressure rebooking text

Hi [Name] — quick check-in on your [vehicle]. It’s been a while since the last [service], so I wanted to see if you’d like me to hold a spot for the next one. No pressure if the timing isn’t right.

Keep it personal, relevant to the customer's last service, and easy to ignore without pressure.

What to track

  • Last service date and service type
  • Expected next-service date
  • Due / overdue / rebooked status
  • Last contact date and outcome
  • Booked date and expected ticket
